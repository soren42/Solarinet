Start here

How to read this guide

Five minutes here saves confusion later. The pictures, the badges and the words below are used the same way on every page.

Each task in this guide is a row of pictures. Read the pictures in number order and do what the sentence under each one says. The pictures are simplified drawings of the real screens, so the live screen has more detail and your own machine names.

Tap hereThe blue ring marks the thing to tap or select in that picture.

Look hereA dashed ring points at something to read or check, not tap.

Numbered dotMatches a number in the sentence under the picture.

ColoursGreen is healthy, amber is degraded, red is down, grey is unknown, violet is maintenance, blue is information. Colour is always paired with a word.

Who can do what

Every task shows a badge. Everyone means anyone with an Akoria account can do it. Operator means the account needs operator rights, which Jason grants. Ask Jason marks a step only the administrator can do. If a button does nothing, or a change fails with a message about an operator role, you have hit one of these limits. That is normal, not a fault.

Words you will meet

DashboardThe SolariNet web page at dashboard.akoria.net. Works best on an iPad; it also works on a phone or computer.
Machine, system, hostOne computer on the house network: a server, a Raspberry Pi, a NAS, a switch. The dashboard uses all three words for the same thing.
FleetAll the machines together.
AlertA message that something crossed a limit, for example a disk nearly full. Red is critical, amber is a warning.
Akoria SSOThe house sign-in service. One username and password for the dashboard and the other Akoria services (Task 10).
Client (agent)A small program installed on a machine that reports its health to the dashboard every few seconds.
Inventory, tagThe list of things the house owns. A tag is the sticker with a barcode that identifies one item.
Reimage, provisionWiping a machine and installing a fresh operating system on it over the network, then setting it up automatically.

Before you begin

  • You need an Akoria account. If you do not have one, ask Jason.
  • Use Safari on an iPad for the best fit. Turn the iPad sideways for the maps and the install screens.
  • You must be on the house network (at home, or on the house VPN). The dashboard is not on the public internet.
  • Nothing in this guide can break a machine except Task 6 and Task 8, which wipe one on purpose and say so loudly.

Task 1

Sign in to the dashboard

Everyone

The dashboard is a web page at dashboard.akoria.net. You sign in once with your Akoria account, and it keeps you signed in while you use it.

  1. 1

    On your iPad, open Safari and type dashboard.akoria.net in the address bar. You must be at home or on the house VPN.

  2. 2

    The sign-in screen appears. Tap Sign in with Akoria SSO. The username and password boxes below it are for emergency local accounts, which most people do not have.

  3. 3

    Safari moves to the Akoria sign-in page. Type your Akoria username or email and your password, then tap Sign In.

  4. 4

    If your account has a second step, Akoria asks for it now: a six-digit code from your authenticator app, or a passkey with Face ID or Touch ID. Task 10 explains how to set these up.

  5. 5

    You are back on the dashboard, on Fleet Overview. A green All systems nominal banner means nothing needs you. Task 2 explains the rest of this page.

  6. 6

    Optional, but handy: tap Safari's Share button, then Add to Home Screen, then Add. SolariNet gets its own icon and opens full screen like an app.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Invalid or expired SSO login attemptThe Akoria sign-in took longer than 10 minutes. Go back to the dashboard and tap Sign in with Akoria SSO again.
The page never loads, or Safari says it cannot find the serverYou are probably not on the house network. Join the house Wi-Fi or the VPN and try again.
Safari warns that the connection is not privateThe dashboard uses a house certificate that Safari does not know yet. Task 9 explains this; if the warning is on dashboard.akoria.net itself, ask Jason before continuing.
You were signed out by yourselfSessions end after 30 minutes with no activity, and an Akoria sign-in lasts at most one hour. Sign in again.
You forgot your passwordTask 10, step 6.

Task 2

Is everything OK? Check the whole network

Everyone

One glance at Fleet Overview answers the usual question. The rest of the page tells you what, where and how bad.

  1. 1

    Open Fleet Overview (the house icon, and the page you land on). Read the banner first: green All systems nominal means everything is fine.

  2. 2

    A red Needs attention banner lists what is wrong, worst first: machines that are down, then critical and warning alerts. Tap a line to jump to its cause.

  3. 3

    The four tiles count the fleet: Systems, Operational, Degraded and Down. Tap a tile to show only those machines.

  4. 4

    In the Heatmap, every square is one machine, grouped by network. Green is up, red is down 1, amber is degraded 2, and a dimmed square 3 stopped reporting. Long-press a square to see its name.

  5. 5

    The Live pill confirms the page updates by itself. Heatmap · Table · Cards are three views of the same machines; Table is easiest on a phone. The chips filter by state, so tapping Down shows only the down machines.

  6. 6

    Topology Map draws the network: the gateway at the top, switches below, machines and access points under them. A red dot is a machine with a problem. Switch between Network and Monitoring at the top. The map cannot be zoomed, so use an iPad sideways.

  7. 7

    A number on the Alerts bell means open alerts. Task 4 shows how to read and acknowledge them. If the bell has no number and the banner is green, you are done.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Every square is grey (Unknown) or the Live pill is missingThe dashboard cannot reach its own server, or the server cannot reach the machines. Wait a minute, then reload. If it stays grey, ask Jason.
One square is dimmedThat machine stopped reporting, usually because it is switched off or unplugged. If it should be on, check its power and network cable, then tell Jason.
A DEMO DATA banner is showingThe page is showing sample data because the real data failed to load. Reload; if it persists, ask Jason.

Task 3

Check one machine

Everyone

Every monitored machine has its own page. This is where you look when a square on the heatmap is not green, or when someone asks "is the NAS busy?".

  1. 1

    On Fleet Overview, tap the machine's square (or its row in Table view, or its card). You can also press ⌘K on a keyboard and type its name.

  2. 2

    The machine's page opens. The strip under its name is the hero: whether it is up, its address, what it does, its operating system, how long it has been running and when it last reported. A report a few seconds old is healthy.

  3. 3

    The criticality chip next to the name says how much the machine matters, from Ignore to Vital. A Vital machine that goes down sounds the wall panel (Task 5). Only operators can change it.

  4. 4

    Five gauges show the machine's load right now: CPU, memory, the fullest disk, swap and network traffic. Red on the disk gauge, as here, means it is almost full.

  5. 5

    Scroll down for the 15-minute trend of CPU and memory. A short spike is normal; a line pinned at the top for the whole window is worth mentioning to Jason.

  6. 6

    The buttons at the top right are for operators. Survey now asks for a fresh reading and Push config re-sends settings; both are harmless. Retire removes the machine from the roster, so leave that one to Jason.

    Operator

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Last reported is minutes or hours agoThe machine has stopped talking to the dashboard. It may be off, unplugged or asleep. Check it physically, then tell Jason if it should be running.
The page is called System detail and has no gaugesThat machine has no client installed, so it is only watched from outside by ping and service checks. Task 7, step 5 explains how a client gets installed.
A gauge is redNot an emergency by itself. If an alert is open for it, Task 4. Otherwise mention it to Jason.

Task 4

Read and acknowledge an alert

EveryoneOperator

An alert is SolariNet saying "something crossed a line". Anyone can read them; acknowledging needs an operator account.

  1. 1

    The Alerts bell in the sidebar shows how many alerts are open. Tap it. (On a narrow screen, open the menu with the ☰ button first.)

  2. 2

    The Active board lists alerts nobody has acknowledged. History holds the acknowledged and the long-cleared ones. The chips count Critical, Warning and Info.

  3. 3

    Read one row: the colour bar 1, the machine 2, the rule and what it saw 3, and the age 4. Tap the machine's name to open its page (Task 3).

  4. 4

    When you have looked at it, tap Ack on that row. This records that a person has seen it. It does not switch anything off.

    Operator
  5. 5

    A message confirms Acknowledged — rule and the row moves to History. Ack all at the top does this for every active alert after a confirmation.

  6. 6

    Acknowledging here does not silence the wall panel's alarm. The panel has its own acknowledge (Task 5), and messages already sent to phones are not recalled.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Ack failed or requires an authenticated operator roleYour account can read alerts but not acknowledge them. Tell Jason what you saw; he can acknowledge it.
The same alert comes back every few minutesSomething is flapping between fine and broken. Acknowledging does not stop it; the machine needs attention. Tell Jason.
The alert says Fleet / probesA check from the outside failed (for example a website on a machine stopped answering). Treat it like any other alert.

Task 5

Read the wall panel and stop its alarm

Everyone

The small LED panel on the wall mirrors the dashboard so nobody has to open an iPad to know the house is fine. When something critical breaks, it is what tells you.

  1. 1

    The panel is a grid of 53 × 11 lights. The colours mean exactly what they mean on the dashboard: green healthy, amber degraded, red down, blue information. Mostly green means all is well.

  2. 2

    It has four themes, each with three screens, and it rotates through them unless someone pins one. Press A, B, C or D on the panel to jump to that theme. D1, the pool bars, is the resting screen.

  3. 3

    The other buttons: LUX+ and LUX− change brightness, VOL+ shows the on-panel help, VOL− makes the alarm quieter, ZZZ sends the display to sleep. None of them can break anything.

  4. 4

    The alarm goes off when a critical alert opens, when too much of a group of machines is down, or when a Vital machine goes down. It is the only part of SolariNet that makes a sound.

  5. 5

    To acknowledge it at the wall, press any button on the panel. The tone and beacon stop for this problem. A new problem starts a new alarm.

  6. 6

    Or acknowledge from your iPad: open Panel in the sidebar and tap Acknowledge under Alarm. The page also shows the twelve screens live and the panel's state.

    Operator
  7. 7

    Two words to know. NO DATA: the panel works but the dashboard is not feeding it; it recovers by itself. LINK LOST: the panel has lost its computer; reseat the USB cable and tell Jason.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
The alarm is sounding and you cannot find the causePress any panel button to stop the tone, then open the dashboard: the red Needs attention banner on Fleet Overview names the problem (Task 2).
The panel is darkSomeone pressed ZZZ, or the panel is asleep. Press any button. If it stays dark, check its power.
Testing the alarmThe test is loud and real. Warn the household first, and leave testing to Jason.

Task 6

Install a new server from scratch

OperatorAsk Jason

SolariNet installs a machine over the network: you choose it and an operating system on the iPad, switch the machine on, and it builds itself. Nothing is typed on the machine.

  1. 1

    Check the list. The dashboard will only offer machines that are in Inventory with a known CPU type and a recorded network address. Task 8 shows how a machine gets there. A reimage wipes the disk.

    Ask Jason
  2. 2

    Put the machine where it will live. Plug in a network cable 1 to the house switch (Wi-Fi will not do for an install) and power 2. Leave it off for now.

  3. 3

    On the iPad, turned sideways, open Manage → Images & Stacks. Make sure the mode at the top is Reimage (wipe + install).

  4. 4

    Pick the machine in the Target list. If it shows a ? and CPU architecture unknown, tap Set and choose its type; this fixes every machine of that model.

  5. 5

    Choose an image, the operating system to install. Pick one marked sha256 ✓. Greyed rows cannot be used and say why. Keep Compatible only on so you only see images that fit this machine.

  6. 6

    Tick the stacks, the software to set up afterwards; the ones marked always are added for you. When the dark bar at the bottom shows no blocker, tap Stage reinstall.

  7. 7

    Read the summary. Type the machine's name exactly as shown, then tap Stage reinstall. The page confirms Reinstall of new-box staged on its network address.

  8. 8

    Now switch the machine on. It asks the house install server for its instructions, downloads the operating system and installs it on its own. Leave it alone; this takes a while. A screen, if attached, shows text scrolling.

  9. 9

    At its first boot the machine asks to join. Open Manage → Provisioning: it appears in the Enrollment queue. Check the name and address, then tap ✓ Sign. Tap Deny for anything you do not recognise.

    Ask Jason
  10. 10

    The page confirms CSR signed — new-box enrolled as client. The stacks install themselves next, and Config convergence turns to Converged.

  11. 11

    Back on Fleet Overview, the new machine has its own green square. Tap it to see its page (Task 3). Done.

Who does what

Everything on the dashboard side of this task needs an operator account. Setting a machine to boot from the network, and signing it in to the fleet, are Jason's jobs unless he has said otherwise.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
The machine is not in the Target listIt has no network address on record, or no hardware model. Task 8 steps 5 and 6 add it; or ask Jason.
Target CPU architecture unknown will not go awayTap Set next to the machine, or open Inventory, find the hardware model and set its CPU architecture there.
Every image is greyed outThe images are the wrong type for this machine, or have not been downloaded. Jason refreshes images.
The machine boots into its old system instead of installingIt did not boot from the network. The boot order in its firmware needs changing; ask Jason.
Nothing appears in the Enrollment queueThe install has not finished, or the machine cannot reach the server. Give it 20 minutes, then check the machine's screen if it has one.

Task 7

Add a machine that is already running

OperatorAsk Jason

A machine that already works, a NAS or a printer for instance, should not be reinstalled. Adopting it tells SolariNet to watch it as it is.

  1. 1

    Open Manage → Discovery. Type the network range in the box at the top right, for example 10.1.0.0/24 (ask Jason which one), and tap Scan. Wait for Scan complete.

  2. 2

    The list shows what the scan found: address, name if known, the maker. Find your machine and tap + Monitor on its row.

  3. 3

    In Monitor system, check the Display name, choose a Classification (server, appliance, network, iot, host or other) and a Pool. Leave the ticks as they are unless you know a service should not be checked. Tap Monitor system.

  4. 4

    The page confirms Adopted new-box — 4 target(s). From now on the dashboard pings it and checks its services from outside. It appears on Systems and Reachability. For many machines, this is all you need.

  5. 5

    For full detail (CPU, memory, disks, Task 3), install the SolariNet client. Open Manage → Provisioning, find Deploy client to a host, type the login as user@address and tap Deploy.

    Ask Jason
  6. 6

    Watch the log under the form. This only works if the machine already accepts Jason's SSH key and allows sudo without a password, so in practice Jason runs this step.

  7. 7

    When the log finishes, the machine asks to join: it appears in the Enrollment queue (unless it was signed automatically). Check the name and address, then tap ✓ Sign.

    Ask Jason
  8. 8

    On Fleet Overview the machine now has a square, and its page shows gauges and a trend like any other client.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Enter a CIDR, e.g. 192.168.1.0/24The range must be written as address/size. Ask Jason for the house ranges; 10.1.0.0/24 is the core network.
The scan is refused as too largeA range can hold at most 4,096 addresses (a /20). Scan a smaller one.
The machine is not in the listIt is off, on another network, or does not answer. Check it is on and wired, then scan again.
Deploy fails at onceThe machine does not accept the SSH key or asks for a sudo password. Ask Jason.
The machine shows up but with no gaugesIt was adopted (watched from outside) but has no client. Steps 5 to 7 add one.

Task 8

From a barcode on the box to a running server

OperatorAsk Jason

Everything the house owns is tracked from the moment it arrives. This task follows one box all the way to a green square on the dashboard.

  1. 1

    Open Manage → Tags & Scan on the iPad 3. The scanner 2 is paired like a keyboard, so you do not need to tap the scan box first. Scan the barcode on the box 1. (The iPad's camera cannot be used here.)

  2. 2

    The code is new, so Receive new stock opens. Choose the Model, check Code type and Serial, pick the Location (the armed bin is preselected), enter the Quantity and Unit cost.

  3. 3

    If the model is not in the list, tap + New model and fill in Make, Model, Type and the CPU architecture 1. The CPU type matters: without it the machine cannot be reimaged later, and it can only be set here or on the hardware model, not on the item itself.

  4. 4

    Tap Receive + tag. The item is recorded, gets an AKO- tag, and Print label opens. Download the PNG or PDF, print it, stick it on the machine, then tap Mark printed.

  5. 5

    Unpack and connect the machine (Task 6, step 2), switch it on, then adopt it: Discovery → Scan → + Monitor → Monitor system (Task 7, steps 1 to 4). This puts the machine, with its network address, into the records.

  6. 6

    Tie the tag to the machine: open Inventory, find the item (search its AKO- tag), open its Components tab and tap Link entity. Search the machine's name, select it and tap Link entity. The page confirms Entity association saved.

  7. 7

    Now install it: Images & Stacks → Reimage, exactly as Task 6 steps 3 to 7. The machine is in the Target list because it now has a model, a CPU type and a network address.

    Ask Jason
  8. 8

    Boot it from the network, sign it in the Enrollment queue (Task 6, steps 8 and 9), and it appears on Fleet Overview. The tag on its case and the square on the dashboard are now the same machine.

Honest limits

The CPU architecture can no longer be set on an item form; it lives on the hardware model. And after adopting a machine, its network address may take a moment, or a nudge from Jason, to reach the records that Images & Stacks reads. If the machine is missing from the Target list, that is why.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Receive new stock did not open; the code was looked up insteadThe code is already known: it belongs to a product type. Use Inventory → Catalog to record another item of that type.
Scanning does nothingA dialog or the search palette is open and is swallowing the keystrokes. Close it and scan again. Scanning also needs an operator account.
The machine is not in the Target list on Images & StacksIts network address has not reached the records yet, or the model has no CPU type. Do steps 5 and 6 again, check the model, then ask Jason.
The label printer is not in the Label profile listDownload the PDF and print it from any printer; the size is on the file.

Task 9

Trust the house certificate (Akoria CA)

EveryoneAsk Jason

House websites use certificates signed by the house's own authority, chlorine. Teach your device to trust it once and the security warnings go away for those sites.

  1. 1

    A certificate is how a device decides a website is genuine. Public sites are vouched for by companies your device already knows; house services are vouched for by the Akoria Root CA. Trusting it once removes the warnings for those services.

  2. 2

    Ask Jason to send you the Akoria profile by AirDrop or Messages and tap Accept. (Alternatively, open ca.akoria.net/roots.pem in Safari; the profile is the easier route.)

    Ask Jason
  3. 3

    Safari asks Do you want to allow this? Tap Allow, then Close on the Profile Downloaded message.

  4. 4

    Open Settings → General → VPN & Device Management, tap Akoria Root CA, then Install (enter your passcode), Install again, and Done. Not Verified on this screen is normal for a house certificate.

  5. 5

    Then Settings → General → About, scroll to the bottom, Certificate Trust Settings. Switch on Akoria Root CA and tap Continue. This second step is the one people forget.

  6. 6

    Check it: open ca.akoria.net in Safari. A padlock and no warning means it worked. The certificate's fingerprint is public and shown here in case Jason asks you to compare it.

  7. 7

    On a Mac: double-click the file to open it in Keychain Access, put it in the System keychain, double-click Akoria Root CA, open Trust and set When using this certificate to Always Trust. Enter your password to save.

  8. 8

    On Windows: rename the file so it ends in .crt, double-click it, choose Install Certificate, Local Machine, then Place all certificates in the following store and pick Trusted Root Certification Authorities. Finish, then restart the browser.

Never do this for a stranger

Trusting a root certificate is a big deal: it lets whoever holds it vouch for any website. Only ever install the Akoria one, only from Jason or from ca.akoria.net, and remove any other profile someone asks you to install.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Safari still warns on dashboard.akoria.net or the sign-in pageExpected for now: those two use their own certificates, not Akoria's. Ask Jason before tapping through a warning on them.
The toggle is missing from Certificate Trust SettingsThe profile was not installed (step 4). Go back to Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.
Firefox still warns on a Mac or PCFirefox keeps its own list. Ask Jason; there is a Firefox policy file for it.
You are asked to type a password you do not haveInstalling on a Mac or PC needs that computer's administrator password. Ask whoever set the computer up.

Task 10

Your Akoria account

EveryoneAsk Jason

Akoria SSO (single sign-on) is the house login service, run on sso.akoria.org. This task is about looking after your own account.

  1. 1

    Akoria SSO is one sign-in for the house: the SolariNet dashboard, Forgejo (where the code and documents live) and the account console itself all use it. Jason creates the account; it is yours from then on.

  2. 2

    First time: sign in with the username and temporary password Jason gave you. Akoria asks you to choose a new password. Pick a long one you do not use anywhere else, and save it in your password manager.

  3. 3

    To manage your account open sso.akoria.org:8443/realms/akoria/account/ and sign in. Under Account security → Signing in you can add a passkey or an authenticator application. These screens are Akoria's standard ones; if the names differ slightly, follow what you see.

  4. 4

    Passkey (recommended on Apple devices): tap Set up next to Passkey, then Continue when the iPad offers to save one. It lives in your iCloud Keychain, so the same passkey works on your iPhone. Signing in afterwards is Face ID or Touch ID, no password.

  5. 5

    Authenticator application (works everywhere): tap Set up, scan the square code with an authenticator app on your phone, and type the six-digit code it shows. From then on Akoria asks for the current code after your password.

  6. 6

    Forgot your password? On the Akoria sign-in page tap Forgot Password?, enter your username or email and tap Submit. Follow the link in the email. If no email arrives within a few minutes, ask Jason to reset it.

    Ask Jason
  7. 7

    To sign out of the dashboard, tap the round initial at the top right and choose Log out. On a shared device also sign out of the account console, because the Akoria sign-in may otherwise let the next person straight back in.

If something goes wrong

You seeWhat to do
Invalid username or passwordCheck caps lock and try once more. After five wrong tries in 15 minutes Akoria pauses sign-in for that name; wait, then try again.
Akoria asks for a code and you have lost the phoneAsk Jason. He can remove the old authenticator from your account so you can set up a new one.
You signed in but the dashboard says you are a viewerYour account is not in an operator group. That is a setting Jason makes, not something you did wrong.

Task 11

Find the manuals and get help

Everyone

This guide is deliberately short. When you want the whole story, the three full manuals are one tap away, and Jason is one message away.

  1. 1

    All the documentation is at akoria.net/docs/. This guide is the first card. The User's Manual explains every screen in depth when you want more than a quick start.

  2. 2

    Inside a manual: 1 opens the contents, 2 searches every word, 3 switches light and dark, 4 prints or saves a PDF. The PDF button on the portal downloads a ready-made copy.

  3. 3

    When something is wrong, tell Jason three things: the exact words you saw, which machine and when, and what you did. A photo of the screen is perfect. For bigger problems, Jason tracks them as issues on Forgejo.

  4. 4

    Nobody from the house will ever ask you for a password or a code, not even Jason. If a message does, it is not from him. Machine names, screenshots and times are always fine to share.
